You put a question to ChatGPT that a customer would ask. Three names come out. Yours is missing. Your competitor's is at the top.

That stings, certainly when you know your work is at least as good. Your competitor is mentioned in ChatGPT and you are not. What do they do differently?

The answer is almost never exciting. They have no special technique and they do not pay for it. In most cases they have simply written down more, and more clearly, what they do. The good news is that you can see exactly what, and that you can catch up.

This article contains an analysis you can do in an hour.

## Why an AI system chooses them

An AI system does not choose a company because it is larger, older or more expensive. It chooses companies about which it can read enough to back up a recommendation.

That comes down to four things. It can find the website. It understands what the company does and where. There is an answer to the question that was asked. And other sources confirm the story. The background is in [how ChatGPT decides which companies to mention](#art_hoe_chatgpt_bepaalt_welke_bedrijven_het_noemt).

Your competitor does at least one of those four things better than you. The analysis below shows which.

## Step 1: establish which questions they win

Do not start with their website. Start with the questions.

Take ten questions your customers ask. Put them to ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, without naming names. Note per question who is mentioned. The method is in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

You get a table with three kinds of questions:

- **Questions where you are both mentioned.** You do not need to do anything here.
- **Questions where they are mentioned and you are not.** This is your work.
- **Questions where neither of you is mentioned.** This is your opportunity. Here you can be the first.

Most people only look at the second group. The third is at least as interesting, because there you do not have to catch up with anyone.

Also note who else is mentioned. Often there are companies among them you did not regard as a competitor. A national chain, a comparison site, a company from a town further along. Those are the parties you compete with in AI, whoever you come across in real life.

## Step 2: find out which page is used

This is the step that makes the difference.

For each question your competitor wins, you want to know which of their pages the system uses. Many systems show their sources. If not, ask: "Which sources do you base this recommendation on?"

Open that page. It is rarely the homepage. Usually it is a specific page that is precisely about the question: a page about one service in one town, an article about cost, a description of a project.

Look at four things.

1. **The heading.** Does it resemble the question you asked?
2. **The first paragraph.** Is the answer in it?
3. **The details.** Are prices, terms, towns or examples named?
4. **The length.** Is it a few sentences, or a full explanation?

Then go to your own website and look for the page that answers the same question. In most cases it is not there.

> You rarely lose to a better page. You lose to a page that does not exist on your side.

## Step 3: count what they have

Now look at their website as a whole. You do not have to read everything. Count.

**How many pages about services do they have?** One page with a list, or a separate page per service?

**Do they have pages per town or region?** And is there something on them, or only the place name?

**Do they have a blog or knowledge base?** How many articles, and when was the last one?

**Do they have frequently asked questions?** Where are they, and are the answers concrete?

**Do they show projects?** With text, or only photos?

A quick way to get an impression: search in a search engine for the word site, a colon and their domain name. You see roughly how many pages are known. Do the same for your own website.

The difference is often large. They have eighty pages, you have twelve. That says nothing about quality. It does mean they have eighty chances of being the answer to a question, and you have twelve.

## Step 4: see what others say about them

Go from their website to the rest of the internet.

**Reviews.** How many do they have, how recent, and what is in them? Read ten. Which words come back? Those are often the words an AI system uses to describe them.

**Mentions.** Search for their company name in quotation marks. Where are they mentioned? A trade association, a supplier, the local paper?

**Their business profile.** Is it complete? Which category did they choose, which services are in it, do they respond to reviews?

Also ask the system directly: "What do customers say about [their name]?" and put the same question about yourself. The difference between the two answers shows how much material a system has on each of you. More on this in [reviews and mentions: why AI trusts others](#art_reviews_en_vermeldingen_waarom_ai_op_anderen_vertrouwt).

## Step 5: compare how you are described

Ask ChatGPT: "What does [their company name] do and what are they good at?" Then put the same question about your own company.

Put the answers side by side. You often see one of these three patterns.

**They get a specific answer, you get a general one.** "Specialised in renovating flat roofs of commercial buildings in the Zwolle region" against "a company that carries out roofing work". The system knows what they do. About you it only knows the sector.

**They get an answer, you get doubt.** "I could find limited information about this company." Then there is too little to read about you.

**You both get something, but yours is wrong.** Then outdated or conflicting information is standing somewhere. See [AI says something wrong about my company](#art_ai_zegt_iets_verkeerds_over_mijn_bedrijf).

## What you usually find

In the comparisons we make, the same pattern keeps coming back. The competitor who is mentioned does one or more of these five things.

1. **They have a page per service.** Not a list, but explanation.
2. **They name prices or ranges.** Most companies do not, so whoever does is quoted.
3. **They name towns.** In sentences and with projects.
4. **They publish regularly.** Their latest article carries a date from this month.
5. **They have reviews with text.** Customers describe what was done.

None of this is secret or difficult. It is work they have done and you have not yet.

## What not to do

**Taking over their texts.** It is tempting, and it does not work. A system that finds the same text twice chooses the original. On top of that it is not allowed.

**Copying their structure blindly.** If they have twenty pages per town with the same text, that is not an example to follow. Look at what works for them, not at everything they have.

**Writing about them.** A page in which you explain why you are better than competitor X rarely works well. You name them and give them attention in doing so. Write about your own work.

**Trying to catch up in one go.** Whoever publishes fifty thin pages in a weekend has fifty pages with nothing in them. Five good ones work better.

**Staring at one competitor.** Next time someone else is at the top. It is about the questions, not about them.

## What to do instead: answer the question better

You do not have to imitate them. You have to answer the same question, and do it better. Better means three things here.

**More concrete.** They write that a roof renovation "depends on the situation". You write what it costs per square metre, for which kind of roof, and what the three things are that push the price up.

**Your own.** They write what is on every website. You write what you see in practice. That in houses from the seventies in your region the roof edge is often the weak point. That with a leak you always check the vents first. That is nowhere else.

**More honest.** They only write advantages. You also write when something is not a good idea, or for whom you are not the right party. A system looking for a balanced answer gets more out of a balanced source.

How to build such an answer is covered in [an FAQ page AI can quote](#art_veelgestelde_vragen_pagina_die_ai_kan_citeren).

## Start with the questions nobody holds

In step 1 you found questions where neither of you is mentioned. The system names a national party or only gives general advice.

Those are the questions where you see results fastest. You do not have to push anyone out. You only have to be the first with a good answer.

We noticed ourselves how that works. On our own website we published a few articles for one trade, carpenters. Nobody else had written about that subject for that group. Five to six weeks later two enquiries from carpenters came in within one week.

So make two lists. The questions they win, to catch up on. The questions nobody wins, to take. Start with the second.

## A plan for six weeks

**Week 1.** Do the analysis. Ten questions, three systems, two competitors. Note which pages are used.

**Week 2.** Get your basics in order. What you do, for whom and where, on your homepage. Your business profile complete.

**Week 3 and 4.** Write four pages about questions where nobody is mentioned.

**Week 5 and 6.** Write four pages about questions your competitor wins. More concrete, your own and more honest than theirs.

**After that.** Measure again and carry on. Allow a few weeks before you see anything. How long exactly is covered in [how long until AI mentions your company](#art_hoe_lang_duurt_het_voordat_ai_je_bedrijf_noemt).

## Keep tracking

Your competitor is not standing still. If they notice it works, they carry on. And others join in.

So it pays to repeat this analysis. Not every week, but every quarter. Which questions do they win now? Have new names been added? Which pages have they added?

By hand that is an hour per quarter. We do it daily. For every client we track one or more competitors and ask the same questions for them as for the client. Where they win and the client does not decides what gets written that week. How many competitors we track depends on the [plan](#prijzen).

## In summary

- A competitor who is mentioned has almost always written down more, and more clearly, what they do.
- See per question which of their pages is used. Usually that page does not exist on your side.
- Take nothing over. Answer the same question more concretely, with your own experience and more honestly.
- Start with the questions where nobody is mentioned yet.
- Repeat the comparison every quarter.
