Anyone who typed "strengthen my position on Google" a few years ago received advice about keywords, links and loading time. That advice has not become wrong. It is just no longer the whole story.

Since May 2025 Google has also shown AI Overviews in the Netherlands: a written answer above the regular results. In October 2025 AI Mode was added, a separate tab in which you hold a conversation with Google. The ten blue links still exist, but they sit lower on the page more and more often.

In this article we explain what that means for your position and what you can do today. Without tricks, because those do not work here.

## What has changed on the results page

A search can now be answered in three ways.

**The regular results.** The list of links you know. For many searches, certainly short and commercial ones, this is still the most important part of the page.

**The AI Overview.** A summary at the top, written by Google, with links to the pages the answer is based on. It mainly appears for questions that need explanation. Google only shows it when it thinks it adds something, so by no means for every search.

**AI Mode.** A conversation with Google in which you ask follow up questions. Meant for questions that involve comparing or reasoning.

For you as a business owner that means the following. You can be in position one and still only come into view halfway down the screen. And the other way round: you can be in position six and still be named as a source in the AI Overview. Position and visibility are no longer the same thing.

## What Google says about it

It is rare for Google to be clear, but here it is. The [documentation for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) states that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. You do not need a special file, separate markup or new technology.

What is required: your page has to be indexed by Google and eligible to be shown in the results with a snippet. Beyond that Google lists the things that have applied for a long time. Make sure your pages are reachable, put important content in text, link to your own pages within your website and keep your business profile current.

It also contains something that explains a lot. For an AI answer, Google runs several searches behind the scenes on subtopics of the question. As a result, different and more pages come into view than with a regular search. So a page does not need to rank at the top for the main question to be used. It has to be the best answer to a part of the question.

That is good news for smaller companies. You do not have to win the battle for the broad keyword. You have to have the best explanation of one piece.

## Six things that strengthen your position now

### 1. Answer the question in the first two sentences

Both the regular results and the AI answers look for a piece of text that answers the question directly. Many pages start with an introduction about the company and only get to the point after three paragraphs.

Turn it round. Put the question in the heading and the answer directly beneath it. Only then the explanation, the exceptions and the background. Whoever wants to read on will. Whoever only wants the answer has it, and so does Google.

### 2. Make one good page per topic

A "Services" page with eight bullet points ranks for nothing. Eight pages that each explain one service rank for eight topics.

Each page answers what a customer wants to know: what it is, who it is for, what it costs, how it goes and where you do it. That is not sales copy. It is the explanation you also give on the phone.

### 3. Write about the parts of the question

Because Google splits a question up, it pays to know the parts. Take "having solar panels installed". Beneath it sit questions about cost, payback time, the state of the roof, permits and maintenance. Whoever answers each of those well comes into view at far more moments than whoever only has the main page.

How to find those questions is covered in [what questions do customers ask AI](#art_welke_vragen_stellen_klanten_aan_ai).

### 4. Keep your Google Business Profile current

For local companies this is the part that yields the most for the least effort. Your profile decides whether you appear on the map, and Google itself names it as a source you should keep current.

Check four things: are your opening hours correct, is your service area in it, are your services filled in and do you respond to reviews. More about working locally in [getting found locally in AI](#art_lokaal_gevonden_worden_in_ai).

### 5. Make sure everything is in text

Google states it explicitly: important content belongs in text. Prices in an image, services in a video without a description, a menu as a photo: it looks good and it does not count.

This also applies to data you pass to Google with special markup. It has to match what is visibly on the page. Adding markup for something a visitor cannot read works against you.

### 6. Keep adding

A website that grows has more pages every month that can answer a question. It is the dullest advice in this article and also the advice that makes the difference. The companies we see rising do nothing special. They do it every week.

## What no longer works

A few habits from the past now cost more than they deliver.

- **Repeating keywords.** A text that says "plumber Utrecht" ten times reads badly and is not quoted. Write it down clearly once.
- **Pages for every town with the same text.** Twenty pages where only the place name differs add nothing. One good page per region with real information about that region does.
- **Long texts for the sake of length.** Length is not a goal. An answer of a hundred words that is right beats a thousand words that circle around it.
- **Buying links.** It was already risky and it yields less and less. One real mention by a party that knows you is worth more than fifty bought ones.

## Will I get fewer visitors because of AI answers?

That is the concern we hear most, and it is justified. If Google gives the answer itself, some people no longer click through.

That mainly applies to simple questions. "What time does the hardware shop close?" That visitor was not going to buy anything anyway. For questions tied to a decision it is different. Someone looking for a builder reads the overview and then clicks through to the companies that are named. That visitor is further along in the choice than someone who clicks a random link from a list of ten.

Our experience is that the number of visitors says less than it used to. What matters is the enquiries. How to get more of those is covered in [more leads from your website with better findability](#art_meer_leads_uit_je_website_met_betere_vindbaarheid).

Google does count the traffic from AI answers in Search Console, in the regular performance report. You do not see it separately, but it is in there.

## Google is no longer the only place

Strengthening your position on Google remains worthwhile. It is just no longer enough to look only at Google.

A growing share of your customers puts the question to ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. Those systems partly use other sources. Gemini leans on Google. Claude searches via Brave, as you can read in [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden). ChatGPT uses a mix. Standing well in Google does not automatically mean standing well in the rest.

Fortunately the work largely overlaps. A clear page that answers a question helps everywhere. The difference lies in the measuring. In Google you look at your position. In AI you look at how often you are mentioned across a whole list of questions. More on that in [what is GEO and how does it differ from SEO](#art_wat_is_geo_en_wat_is_het_verschil_met_seo).

## A plan for the next four weeks

You do not have to do everything at once. This is an order that works.

1. **Week 1: the basics.** Go through your business profile. Put one sentence at the top of your homepage and your main pages with what you do, for whom and where.
2. **Week 2: your best page.** Choose the service you earn most from. Rewrite that page so that the five questions customers ask about it are literally answered in it.
3. **Week 3: the parts.** Write three short pieces about parts of the question for that service. Cost, duration and what to look out for are nearly always on target.
4. **Week 4: measure.** Look in Search Console at which searches your pages are now shown for. In addition, test ten customer questions in ChatGPT and Gemini, as described in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

After that you repeat weeks 2 to 4 for your next service. In six months there will be a website that answers nearly everything a customer can ask you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I still need to care about keywords at all?

Yes, as a starting point. A keyword tells you which topic people care about. The work has moved from repeating the word to answering the questions behind it. One clear page that covers the topic properly does more than five pages each chasing a variation of the same phrase.

### How do I see whether I appear in an AI Overview?

Search for the questions your customers ask in a window in which you are not logged in, and look at the sources listed beside the overview. Repeat it a month later, because the sources change. A walk through is in [getting into Google AI Overviews](#art_in_de_ai_overzichten_van_google_komen).

### Is a faster website still important?

A slow website loses visitors, so yes. It is a threshold rather than a lever, though. Once your pages load acceptably on a phone, extra speed delivers far less than one more page that answers a real question.
