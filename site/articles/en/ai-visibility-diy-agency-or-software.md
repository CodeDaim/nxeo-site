You know it has to happen. Customers put their questions to AI, and you want to be mentioned. The next question is practical: who is going to do it?

There are three routes. You do it yourself. You bring in an agency. Or you use software that carries out the work for you. We sell the third, so read this with that in mind. We have done our best to describe all three honestly, because nobody benefits if you choose the wrong one.

## What has to be done

Before you can compare, you need to know what the work consists of. AI visibility is not a single job. It is four tasks that keep coming back.

1. **Measuring.** Which questions do your customers ask, and are you mentioned for them? You have to check that regularly, in several systems.
2. **Choosing.** Which questions do you tackle first? Where does a competitor win and why?
3. **Writing.** Pages, answers and articles that answer the questions.
4. **Publishing and keeping up.** It has to get onto your website, properly formatted, and stay current.

The first time, each task is manageable. It becomes heavy because it comes back every week. When comparing the three routes, that is the question that counts: who keeps it up?

## Route 1: do it yourself

### What it involves

You collect your customers' questions yourself. You test them by hand in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. You write the answers and put them on your website.

Everything you need to know for that is in the articles on this website. How to test is covered in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai). How to collect questions in [what questions do customers ask AI](#art_welke_vragen_stellen_klanten_aan_ai). How to write answers in [an FAQ page AI can quote](#art_veelgestelde_vragen_pagina_die_ai_kan_citeren).

### What it costs

No money. It does cost time. Allow a day to get started and two to four hours per week after that. Writing a page that is good takes most people an hour and a half to two hours. Measuring by hand half an hour per week.

### What it is good at

**Nobody knows your company better.** You know what customers ask, what goes wrong in practice and why you do things the way you do. That is exactly the content that makes the difference.

**You learn what works.** Whoever does it themselves understands it. That is also valuable if you outsource it later.

**It costs nothing.** For a company that is starting out, that sometimes settles it.

### Where it goes wrong

**It stalls.** This is the honest problem. The first three weeks go well. Then a busy period comes, and after that you do not pick it up again. We see it with nearly everyone.

**Measuring by hand is unreliable.** AI answers fluctuate. Whoever asks ten questions once a week sees chance and not a trend. To do it properly you have to measure often, and you cannot keep that up by hand.

**You do not see your own blind spots.** You write about what you consider important, and that is not always what the customer asks.

### Who it suits

Business owners who like writing, who can free up time for it every week and who mainly want to understand it themselves. And anyone who wants to start without spending anything. The first steps can be done by anyone.

## Route 2: an agency

### What it involves

You hire a marketing agency or a specialist. They do research, make a plan, write the content and publish it, or deliver it to your website builder. You have one fixed contact and regular meetings.

### What it costs

An agency bills hours. What you pay depends on the agency and the scope. For ongoing work on content and findability, the amounts in practice are a good deal higher than with software, often many times higher. Always ask what is concretely delivered per month, in numbers.

### What it is good at

**Custom work.** A good agency gets to know your company and your market, and makes a strategy that fits.

**Breadth.** Findability rarely stands apart from the rest. An agency can also improve your website, run adverts and take on your branding.

**Thinking along.** You have someone to test ideas on. For larger companies with complicated questions that is worth a lot.

**Experience with hard cases.** Several locations, several languages, a market with heavy competition. You need people for that.

### Where it goes wrong

**The price.** For a company with five staff an agency is often not feasible. And whoever takes a small package gets few hours and therefore little work.

**Hours go into meetings.** Part of what you pay goes to alignment, reports and presentations. That is not wrong, but it is not content on your website.

**Not every agency measures in AI.** Many agencies are strong in classic findability and have not yet set up measurement in AI systems properly. Ask about it. How do you measure whether I am mentioned, in which systems and how often?

**Dependence.** Pay attention to who owns the content and where it lives. If everything is on your own website, that is fine.

### Who it suits

Companies with a marketing budget and questions that need custom work. Anyone who wants a new website, a campaign and findability in one hand. And anyone who wants to think things through with someone who knows the market.

## Route 3: software that carries out the work

### What it involves

Here we have to make a distinction, because there are two kinds of software.

**Software that measures.** A dashboard that shows how often you are mentioned and what you should do. You then do the work yourself. This solves the measuring and not the keeping up.

**Software that measures and carries out.** The system measures, determines what is missing, writes the content and publishes it on your website. You approve. This is what NXEO does.

This section is about the second kind.

### What it costs

A fixed amount per month. With us that is €99, €179 or €299, depending on how much is made per week and how many competitors we track, plus €195 once for the setup. The full comparison is under [plans](#prijzen).

### What it is good at

**It carries on.** This is the most important point. Software does not get tired and has no busy period. Something is added every week, even when you do not give it a moment's thought.

**The measuring is reliable.** Daily, in several systems, for you and your competitors. That cannot be done by hand.

**The price.** Because much is automated, it costs a fraction of buying hours.

**You keep control.** Nothing goes online without you having seen it.

### Where it goes wrong

This is the part we do not leave out.

**Less custom work.** Software works with what it knows about your company: the onboarding, your website, the measurements. A writer who spends an afternoon at your premises picks up things a system does not see. The more you supply yourself, the better it gets.

**It is not a strategic partner.** With the entry plan you do not get an adviser who thinks along about your positioning. You get execution.

**It only works in your existing website.** If your website is a technical mess, software does not fix that.

**You still have to approve.** A quarter of an hour per week, but it is not zero. Whoever never looks does not get the best result.

### Who it suits

Companies without a marketing department, that know it has to happen and do not get round to it themselves. Anyone for whom an agency is too expensive and who cannot keep up doing it themselves. More about what it is and is not in [what is NXEO](#art_wat_is_nxeo).

## The three side by side

**Cost.** Do it yourself: no money. Agency: the highest. Software: a fixed low amount.

**Time you put in.** Do it yourself: two to four hours per week. Agency: an hour per week on meetings and approvals. Software: about a quarter of an hour per week.

**Knowledge of your company.** Do it yourself: the best. Agency: good, after a settling in period. Software: as good as what you supply.

**Regularity.** Do it yourself: the weak point. Agency: good, within the hours you buy. Software: the strong point.

**Measuring in AI.** Do it yourself: by hand, limited. Agency: varies per agency. Software: daily.

**Custom work and strategy.** Do it yourself: what you come up with. Agency: the strong point. Software: limited.

## Combinations that work well

It does not have to be a choice for one route. We often see three combinations.

**Start yourself, hand over later.** Do the first steps yourself. Update your business profile, put your service area on your website, write five answers. You then understand what is happening. Hand it over at the moment you notice it stalling.

**Software for the rhythm, yourself for the special pieces.** Let software make the weekly content and now and then write a piece yourself about something only you can tell. A remarkable project, an opinion about your trade. That combination produces the best websites.

**Agency for the foundation, software for what follows.** Have an agency set up your website and positioning properly. That is one time work where custom work pays off. Then use software to keep it up.

## Five questions to help you choose

1. **Do you have two hours to spare every week, and will you keep that up for a year?** Be honest. If so, do it yourself.
2. **Do you have a budget for marketing and a question that is complicated?** Then an agency is worth the money.
3. **Do you know it has to happen and do you not get round to it?** Then software with execution fits.
4. **Is your website in order?** If not, start there. None of the three routes works on a website that cannot be read.
5. **How fast do you need results?** None of the three delivers customers next week. See [how long until AI mentions your company](#art_hoe_lang_duurt_het_voordat_ai_je_bedrijf_noemt).

## What to watch for, whichever route you choose

**Who owns the content?** Everything should be on your own website and remain yours if you stop. Ask beforehand.

**What is delivered?** In numbers. How many pages, how many answers, how often a report.

**How is it measured?** In which systems, with which questions, how often. A report with only positions in Google tells half the story.

**What is promised?** Whoever guarantees position one in ChatGPT promises something that does not exist. Nobody can force what an AI system says.

**How long are you tied in?** And is that reasonable, given the time it takes before you see anything?
