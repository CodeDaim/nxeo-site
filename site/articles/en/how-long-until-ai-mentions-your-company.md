You have written a page, updated your business profile and answered three customer questions. Two days later you put the question to ChatGPT. Nothing. A week later once more. Still nothing.

How long until AI mentions your company? It is a reasonable question, and the honest answer is not what people want to hear. It takes weeks. Sometimes months.

In this article we explain why, what to expect per period and how you can tell it is working before your name appears in an answer.

## The short answer

From what we see with ourselves and with clients, a pattern emerges that is fairly steady.

- **Day 1 to 14:** nothing visible happens. Your pages are found and indexed.
- **Week 3 to 8:** the first shifts. You are mentioned now and then for specific questions.
- **Month 2 to 3:** it becomes a pattern. You appear more often and for more questions.
- **After 3 months:** you have enough measurements to see what has really changed.

Those are not guarantees. They are the terms we count on. Below is why they are what they are.

## Why it cannot go faster

Between the moment you publish something and the moment an AI system mentions you, there are three steps. Each step takes time.

### Step 1: your page has to be found

An AI system that searches the internet uses a search service for that. That search service first has to discover your new page and index it. That does not happen the moment you click publish.

How fast it goes depends on how often the search service visits your website. A website that posts something new every week is visited more often than a website where nothing has happened for three years. With the first, a new page can be known within a few days. With the second it can take weeks.

On top of that, the systems use different search services. Gemini leans on Google. Claude uses Brave, as you can read in [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden). Your page can already be known to one service and not yet to another.

### Step 2: your page has to rank well enough

Being found is not enough. Your page also has to come up for a search. A new page starts without a history. It has no links to it yet and the search service does not yet know how good it is.

For a very specific question about which little has been written, a new page can reach the top quickly. For a broad question about which hundreds of pages exist, it takes longer and may never work with one page.

### Step 3: the system has to choose you

Even when your page is found and comes up, the system has to decide to mention you. In doing so it also looks at other sources. Is your story confirmed somewhere? Are your details correct? One page does not make you a familiar name.

This is the step that asks the most patience. Trust builds up. Every page that is added, every review and every mention strengthens the picture.

## And the model itself?

There is one more slow layer. An AI model knows things from its training, and that training only happens now and then. Between the moment a model is trained and the moment you use it, there is easily six months to a year.

So what you publish today only ends up in that knowledge with a later version of the model, and only if it is on the internet often and clearly enough.

Fortunately you do not have to wait for that. For questions about local companies, prices and recommendations, the systems usually search the internet live. There you come into view sooner. The training is the long term. The live search is what you build on in the first months.

## Three things that set the pace

### 1. How active your website already was

A website that has existed for years and is updated regularly has a head start. Search services know it and come by often. New pages are picked up quickly.

A brand new website, or a website where nothing has happened for a long time, starts more slowly. Allow a few extra weeks there.

### 2. How strong your competitors are

In a region where not a single competitor explains anything on their website, it can go quickly. You are the only one with an answer.

In a market where three parties have been publishing for years, you have to catch up. That is possible, but it takes more pages and more time.

We noticed it ourselves. On our own website we published a few articles about one trade, carpenters. Five to six weeks later two enquiries from carpenters came in within one week. That went quickly because hardly anyone else had written about that subject for that group.

### 3. How regularly you publish

This is the factor most in your own hands. One page has one chance. Ten pages have ten chances, and together they show that you know the subject.

Whoever publishes ten good pages in the first month is further along in three months than whoever publishes one each month. After that: better something every week than a pile once a quarter.

## What you can see before you are mentioned

The hard thing about the first weeks is that it seems as if nothing is happening. There are four signals that show it is working.

**Your pages are indexed.** In Search Console you can see whether Google knows your new pages. That is the first step, and you can check it within a week or two.

**You are shown for new searches.** In the same program you can see for which searches your website appears. If questions are added that fit your new pages, they are being found.

**You appear as a source.** Sometimes you are not yet recommended by name, but a system does use your page as a source for an explanation. That is a forerunner. The system has found you and considers you usable.

**The description improves.** Ask a system what your company does. Is the answer more accurate than a month ago? Then it is reading your new content.

These signals come weeks earlier than a recommendation. Whoever only watches for that recommendation thinks for too long that it is not working.

## Why you should not look every day

AI answers fluctuate. Ask the same question twice and you can get two different sets. Whoever looks every day sees themselves there on Monday and not on Tuesday, and draws conclusions from that which are wrong.

What counts is the average over a longer period. If you are mentioned for a question three out of ten times this month and one out of ten times last month, it is heading the right way. Even if you happen to be missing today.

That is why we measure daily and only draw conclusions after about thirty days. One measurement is noise. Thirty measurements are a line. How to approximate that by hand is covered in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

## What goes faster than you think

Not everything takes weeks. A few things have an almost immediate effect.

**Updating your business profile.** Changes to your Google Business Profile are often visible within a few days, and they carry through to Gemini and to the AI answers in Google.

**Correcting mistakes on existing pages.** A page that is already known and visited often by search services is read again quickly. Adjusting a wrong service area or an old price can carry through within a week or two.

**Removing a block.** If your website was accidentally keeping AI systems or search services out, lifting that can give a jump. Everything that was already there suddenly becomes readable.

So always start with what is already there, and write new material after that.

## What takes longer than you think

**Being mentioned for broad questions.** "Who is the best accountant in the country" is a question where established names are years ahead. Aim at the questions with a town or a specialism in them.

**Setting a wrong picture straight.** If a system thinks it knows something about you from its training, that does not disappear as soon as you adjust your website. It takes until a new version of the model before the old knowledge is replaced. In the meantime the live search does help you. More on that in [AI says something wrong about my company](#art_ai_zegt_iets_verkeerds_over_mijn_bedrijf).

**Trust through others.** Reviews and mentions arrive one at a time. There is no accelerator for that.

## Is there a way to speed it up?

A few things help. None of them is a trick.

1. **Link to your new pages from pages that are already visited.** A link from your homepage to a new article means search services find it sooner.
2. **Submit new pages in Search Console.** You can ask Google to look at a page. That speeds up the first step for Google's systems.
3. **Publish more at the start.** A solid start shows search services that something is happening, so that they come by more often.
4. **Choose specific questions.** The narrower the question, the sooner you can be the best answer.
5. **Share what you write.** An article that is shared on LinkedIn or that a partner links to is discovered sooner.

What does not help: asking the same question every day in the hope that the system gets to know you. That is not how it works. Your conversation has no influence on what someone else gets to see.

## A realistic timeline for yourself

Are you going to do it yourself? Then this is a schedule you can count on.

**Week 1.** Measure where you stand. Update your business profile. Put on your homepage what you do, for whom and where.

**Week 2 to 4.** Write five pages about the questions customers ask most. Link to them from your homepage.

**Week 5 to 8.** Write one page each week. Check in Search Console whether your pages are indexed.

**Week 9 to 12.** Measure again, with the same questions as in week 1. Compare.

**After that.** Carry on with what works. See which questions are still missing.

If you see no change at all after twelve weeks, something else is going on. Usually that is a technical block or a website that cannot be read. The causes are in [why am I not in AI recommendations](#art_waarom_sta_ik_niet_in_de_aanbevelingen_van_ai).

## In summary

- You see the first shifts after four to eight weeks. You have a reliable picture after three months.
- It takes that long because your page has to be found, valued and trusted.
- The pace depends on your website, your competitors and your regularity.
- In the first weeks, watch for the early signs: indexed pages, new searches and a better description.
- Do not look every day. Look at the average per month.
