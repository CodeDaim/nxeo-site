You have tested it. ChatGPT mentions your company neatly for a few questions. Then you put the same question to Gemini, and you do not exist. Or the other way round: Gemini knows you, but gives a description that is wrong.

"Gemini does not mention my company, why?" is a question we get more and more often. That makes sense. Gemini is in Android phones, in Gmail, in Chrome and in Google Search itself. Many people use it without consciously choosing it.

The answer to the question can almost always be traced back to one thing: Gemini looks through Google's glasses. Once you understand what that means, you know where to look.

## Where Gemini gets its answers

Every AI system has two sources. What the model knows from its training, and what it looks up at the moment of the question. The difference between the systems is mainly in that second part.

Gemini belongs to Google and therefore searches in Google. When Gemini backs up an answer with current information, it comes from Google Search. For questions about local companies, information from Google Maps and from business profiles can be added: addresses, opening hours, ratings.

The same technology sits behind the AI Overviews and AI Mode in Google Search. Google itself writes that those answers are produced with a customised version of the Gemini models. So it is one family. What goes well in the AI Overviews usually goes well in Gemini too, and vice versa.

That gives a simple rule:

> If you are unclear to Google, you are invisible to Gemini.

## Why ChatGPT mentions you and Gemini does not

This difference confuses people most, so we will dwell on it for a moment.

ChatGPT does not search the same sources. OpenAI does not disclose exactly which search services it uses, and that has also changed over time. Claude searches somewhere else again, as you can read in [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden).

Each search service has its own picture of the internet. A page that is well in view with one service can be missing or lower with another. On top of that, each model summarises and weighs differently.

The result is that you can have a different outcome per system. That is not a flaw in your test. It is the reason you always have to measure in several systems, as described in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

## The five causes we find most often with Gemini

### 1. Your Google Business Profile is empty or out of date

For local companies this is the first place we look. A profile with only a name and an address tells Google little. A profile with the right category, completed services, a service area, current opening hours, photos and answered reviews tells it a lot.

Pay particular attention to the category. Someone who is listed as a "contractor" and in fact only does roofing will not come into view for questions about roofers.

### 2. Google does not know your pages

It sounds too simple, but it happens. Part of your website is not indexed by Google. That occurs with new pages nothing links to, with pages that have been shielded by accident and with websites that are technically hard to read.

You can check it in a minute. Type into Google the word site, a colon and your domain name, without spaces. You then see which of your pages Google knows. If your main services are missing, you know enough. In Search Console you can see per page why it is or is not indexed.

### 3. There is no answer on your page

Gemini, like the AI Overviews, looks for a piece of text that answers a question. Google writes that for such an answer it runs several searches on subtopics. So your page has to be the best answer to a part of the question.

A page saying "Our services: design, maintenance, renovation" answers nothing. A page that explains what garden maintenance costs per year for a garden of a hundred square metres, and what that depends on, does.

### 4. Your details are not the same everywhere

Google makes connections between what it reads on your website, what is in your business profile and what other websites say about you. If those connections differ, it is not certain it is dealing with the same company.

The classic example: a company moves, updates the website and forgets the profile. Or the name on the shopfront differs from the trade name on the website. To Gemini those are two companies, and neither is convincing.

### 5. Others say too little about you

Reviews weigh heavily with Google for local results, and they give Gemini something to write about. A system that has to explain why it recommends you needs words. It can only write "customers mention the quick response and the clear quote" if customers have written that down somewhere.

More on this in [reviews and mentions: why AI trusts others](#art_reviews_en_vermeldingen_waarom_ai_op_anderen_vertrouwt).

## How to test it the right way

A few things are different with Gemini than with other systems.

**Gemini knows you.** If you are logged in with your Google account, Gemini can take your location and your earlier activity into account. So test in a window in which you are not logged in, or always put the town in your question.

**Also test in Google Search itself.** Ask the same question in Google and see whether an AI Overview appears, and which sources are listed with it. Open AI Mode as well. Because the technology is related, this tells you a lot about how Google sees you.

**Ask on.** Gemini often shows which sources an answer is based on. See which websites those are. If there are comparison sites and business directories among them, you know Gemini is using those for lack of anything better. Are you on those sites?

## What to do about it, in order

1. **Update your business profile.** Right category, all services, service area, opening hours, ten recent photos. This takes an hour and for local companies it is often the biggest step.
2. **Check whether Google knows your pages.** Fix what is missing. Link from your homepage to your main pages.
3. **Make your details match.** Name, address and services on your website, your profile and your social channels.
4. **Rewrite your most important page.** Question in the heading, answer in the first two sentences, explanation below. How to do that is covered in [an FAQ page AI can quote](#art_veelgestelde_vragen_pagina_die_ai_kan_citeren).
5. **Ask for reviews.** Not twenty in one week, but one after each completed job. Ask customers to describe what you did.
6. **Keep adding.** Every new page that answers a question is an extra chance.

The work you do for Gemini is largely the same work that strengthens your position in Google. That is the subject of [strengthen my position on Google](#art_mijn_positie_versterken_op_google).

## Do I need to do anything special for Gemini?

No, and Google says the same about its AI answers. There is no special file you need to place and no separate markup you need. Your pages have to be reachable, be indexed by Google and contain content that is of use to someone.

There is one setting that is worth knowing about. Google has a separate signal with which site owners can indicate whether their content may be used for Gemini. Some security packages and website builders switch on such blocks by default, with the good intention of keeping AI out. In doing so you exclude yourself. If in doubt, ask the party that manages your website whether anything is being blocked.

## How long before Gemini does mention you?

Changes to your business profile are often visible within a few days. New pages first have to be found and indexed by Google. That usually takes a few days to a few weeks, depending on how often Google visits your website.

After that the question is whether your page is the best answer. That depends on what your competitors have published. In a region where nobody explains anything, it can go quickly. In a market where three parties have been publishing for years, it takes longer.

Count on weeks, not days. A fuller answer with what to expect per period is in [how long until AI mentions your company](#art_hoe_lang_duurt_het_voordat_ai_je_bedrijf_noemt).

## What if Gemini mentions me, but wrongly?

That happens, and it is sometimes more annoying than not being mentioned. Gemini says you are open on Sundays when you are not, or names a service you have stopped offering.

The cause almost always lies with a source. Old opening hours in your profile, a page on your website you have forgotten about or a business directory with details from five years ago. Find the source and correct it. The answer follows, although it takes a while. The approach is set out step by step in [AI says something wrong about my company](#art_ai_zegt_iets_verkeerds_over_mijn_bedrijf).

## A short example

A made up example that shows the pattern. A bicycle shop with a workshop is named by ChatGPT when someone asks where to have an electric bike repaired in town. Gemini names three other shops.

The website is fine. It has a clear page about repairs for electric bikes, with the brands the shop services. The business profile, however, lists the shop only under the category for bicycle shops, without a single service filled in, and the opening hours still show the winter schedule from two years ago.

ChatGPT read the website. Gemini leaned on the profile. Filling in the repair services, adding the workshop category and correcting the hours is an hour of work, and it addresses the exact place where the two systems part ways.

## In summary

- Gemini searches in Google. Your visibility in Gemini starts with how well Google understands you.
- The difference with ChatGPT arises because the systems use different search services.
- For local companies the business profile is the first place to look.
- After that comes the content: pages that answer a question directly.
- Special technology is not needed. Regularity is.
