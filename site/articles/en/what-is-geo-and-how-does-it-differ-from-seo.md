Anyone who has looked into online findability lately has come across the abbreviation. GEO. Sometimes also AEO, AIO or LLMO. It sounds like a new profession with new rules, and there are plenty of parties selling it that way.

What is GEO really? The abbreviation stands for generative engine optimisation: making sure your company appears in the answers AI systems write. It is less new than it seems and on a few points it is different after all.

In this article we explain the difference without the abbreviations, because as a business owner you do not need them.

## First the two terms

**SEO** is search engine optimisation. The work you do to get higher in the results of a search engine. The goal is a good place in a list, so that people click on your link.

**GEO** is the work you do to be mentioned in an answer written by an AI system. Think of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and the AI Overviews in Google. The goal is that your name is in the answer, or that your page is used as a source.

The other abbreviations mean roughly the same. AEO stands for answer engine optimisation. LLMO refers to the language models behind the systems. You do not need to remember them.

## The difference in one picture

Imagine a library.

With SEO you ask the librarian where the books about gardening are. They point you to a shelf. You choose a book yourself. Whoever has their book at eye level gets picked most often.

With GEO you ask the librarian how to prune a box hedge. They have read the books and tell you. Perhaps they add which book they found it in. It does not matter which shelf that book is on. What matters is whether they read it, whether they understood it and whether they thought it worth mentioning.

That is the difference. With SEO you win a place. With GEO you win a mention.

## What stays the same

This is the part sellers of GEO prefer to skip. Most of the work is the same.

Google is strikingly clear about it. The [documentation on AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) states that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. No new file is needed and no special markup. The existing basics are enough.

Those basics also apply to the other systems, because they all use a search service to find pages. What counts everywhere:

- **Your pages are reachable.** A system that cannot open your website can do nothing with you.
- **Your content is in text.** Not in images and not hidden behind a click.
- **You are clear about what you do.** Service, audience and service area are stated literally.
- **Your website has a logical structure.** Headings, paragraphs and links between your own pages.
- **Others refer to you.** Mentions and reviews confirm what you say yourself.
- **You keep it current.** Outdated information is used less readily.

So whoever has their findability in Google in good order has a solid base for AI. Whoever does not will not fix it with a trick for AI. What you can do for Google is covered in [strengthen my position on Google](#art_mijn_positie_versterken_op_google).

## What is different

There are four differences that matter.

### 1. People ask different questions

In a search engine people type a few words. "Bookkeeper Amersfoort." To an AI assistant they put a whole question, often with their situation attached. "I have just started as a freelancer in construction and I am looking for a bookkeeper in Amersfoort who also does my tax return. Roughly what does that cost?"

A page made for two keywords does not answer that question. A page that explains what a bookkeeper does and costs for a freelancer who is starting out does.

### 2. The system quotes passages, not pages

A search engine shows your page as a whole. An AI system takes a paragraph out of it and works it into an answer. That means every paragraph has to be able to stand alone.

A paragraph that starts with "As explained above" cannot be used. A paragraph that starts with "Fitting a dormer costs on average" and then gives the answer can. That is why the question in the heading and the answer in the first sentence works so well.

### 3. There is no fixed position

In Google you sit in a place, and that place is roughly the same for most people. An AI answer is written anew every time. Ask the same question twice and you can get two different sets.

So you cannot say you are in position two in ChatGPT. You can only say how often you are mentioned when you ask a question ten or a hundred times.

### 4. Each system searches somewhere else

With SEO, in the Netherlands it comes down to Google in practice. With GEO you are dealing with several systems that each use their own sources. Gemini leans on Google. Claude searches via Brave. ChatGPT uses a mix.

As a result you can stand well in one system and not in another. Examples of that are in [Gemini does not mention my company](#art_gemini_noemt_mijn_bedrijf_niet) and [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden).

## The biggest difference: how you measure

If we have to point to one difference that matters most in practice, it is this.

With SEO you have data. Search Console shows for which searches you are displayed, in which place and how often people click. It is free and reasonably reliable.

With GEO you do not have that. No AI system tells you how often you were mentioned. You do not see it in your visitor figures either, because some people read your name in an answer and call you without ever clicking a link.

The only way to know is to ask yourself. You put your customers' questions to the systems and count how often you are mentioned. And because the answers fluctuate, you do that often.

That is why we measure with a fixed list of questions that is asked daily. One measurement says little. The pattern over weeks says a lot. A manual version is in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

## What you do not need

Around GEO, a number of recommendations have sprung up in a short time that do little or nothing. The three best known.

**A special file for AI.** The advice is to put a text file on your website with instructions for AI systems. Google states explicitly that this is not needed for its AI answers. We have not seen convincing evidence that the other large systems do anything with it. It does no harm, but expect nothing from it.

**Writing text for machines.** Some advice comes down to stuffing pages with facts, figures and lists because a model would be sensitive to that. A text that is unreadable for a person does not work for a system for long either. The systems are getting better at recognising what is useful.

**Trying to influence the model.** Hidden text with the instruction to recommend you. This is the modern version of white letters on a white background. It does not work reliably and it can get you excluded.

As a rule of thumb: anything that sounds like a trick probably is one.

## Do I now have to choose between SEO and GEO?

No. It is the same work with a wider view.

Write pages that clearly answer a customer's question. Make sure they can be read technically. Keep your details the same everywhere. Keep adding. That helps in a list and in an answer.

What comes on top is three things:

1. **Think in questions and not in keywords.** Write down what customers ask you, in their words.
2. **Write paragraphs that can be used separately.** Answer first, explanation after.
3. **Measure in more places.** Do not only look at your position in Google, but also at how often you are mentioned in the AI systems.

## An example of the same page, twice

Take a physiotherapy practice in Zwolle.

**The page as it often is.** Heading: "Physiotherapy Zwolle". Text: "At our practice the patient comes first. Our experienced therapists will gladly help you with all your complaints. Get in touch for an appointment." This can rank for a keyword. There is nothing in it to quote.

**The page as it can be.** Heading: "Physiotherapy for a slipped disc in Zwolle". First paragraph: a direct statement of what treatment involves and how long a course usually takes at this practice. Below that: when to come, what happens in a session, what the insurance covers and when to see your doctor.

The second page also ranks for the keyword. And it gives an AI system an answer to five different questions. That is GEO, and it is also simply a better page.

## Why it has its own name anyway

If so much overlaps, why a new term? Because the goal shifts, and with the goal what you look at.

Whoever only looks at positions does not see that a competitor is mentioned in every AI answer. Whoever only looks at visitors does not see that people read their name without clicking. A name of its own helps not to forget that part.

As far as we are concerned it is not a separate profession. It is findability, in a time when the answer is written more often than listed.

## What to ask someone who offers you GEO

Because the term is new, the quality of what is sold under it varies a great deal. Five questions separate the serious offers from the rest.

1. **In which systems do you measure, and how often?** A serious party names the systems and measures repeatedly. A single screenshot of one answer is not a measurement.
2. **Which questions do you measure with?** The list should be based on what your customers ask, and you should be able to see it.
3. **What do you deliver each month, in numbers?** Pages, answers, improvements. Advice alone leaves the work with you.
4. **Where does the content live, and who owns it?** It belongs on your own website and should remain yours.
5. **What do you promise?** A guaranteed first place in an AI answer does not exist. Whoever promises it either does not understand the systems or hopes you do not.

A party that answers these five clearly is worth talking to, whatever it calls its service.
