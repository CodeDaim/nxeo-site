You have done your work well for years. Customers come back and send others your way. And then someone asks ChatGPT who to call, and three names appear. Yours is not among them. The name of a competitor you rate lower is.

It feels unfair, and in a sense it is. An AI system does not know how good your work is. It only knows what can be read about it. A company that is missing from AI recommendations almost never has a quality problem. It has a readability problem.

Below are the seven causes we come across most when we work out for a company why it is missing. For each cause you will find how to recognise it and what to do about it.

## First: how an AI system chooses whom to mention

A short explanation helps to understand the causes. When someone asks for a recommendation, a system roughly does three things.

It translates the question into a few searches. It fetches the pages that come up for those and reads them. And it writes an answer based on what it finds in those pages, supplemented with what it already knew from its training.

You can drop out at each of those three moments. Your page does not come up. Your page does come up, but there is nothing usable in it. Or there is something usable in it, but others say it more clearly. The extended version is in [how ChatGPT decides which companies to mention](#art_hoe_chatgpt_bepaalt_welke_bedrijven_het_noemt).

## Cause 1: your website does not say what you do

This is by far the most common. Open your own homepage and read the first three sentences. Do they say what you do, for whom and in which region?

Often it says something like: "Welcome to Johnson. For more than thirty years we have stood for quality, service and craftsmanship." No person can do anything with that, and neither can a system. It does not say you renovate bathrooms. It does not say you do so in Apeldoorn.

**How to recognise it:** let someone who does not know your company look at your homepage for five seconds and ask what you do. If they cannot say it in one sentence, a system cannot either.

**What to do:** write one sentence at the top of every important page with service, audience and service area. "We renovate bathrooms for homeowners in Apeldoorn and the surrounding area." Dull, and exactly what is needed.

## Cause 2: the answer to the question is nowhere

People ask AI whole questions. "What does it cost to renovate a bathroom of six square metres?" "How long does it take and can I keep living at home during that time?" A system looks for pages that contain the answer.

Most websites have a "Services" page with a list, and nothing else. The customer's question is not answered anywhere. So the system quotes a competitor who did take the trouble, or a comparison site.

**How to recognise it:** write down the five questions you get most often on the phone and search your own website for the answer. If you cannot find it within half a minute, it is not there for a system either.

**What to do:** create a page or a clear block per question. How to structure that is covered in [an FAQ page AI can quote](#art_veelgestelde_vragen_pagina_die_ai_kan_citeren).

## Cause 3: your text could be anyone's

"We think along with you." "No job too big or too small." "Personal contact comes first." Every competitor has the same sentences. A system that reads ten websites like that has no reason at all to mention you in particular.

What does work is everything only you can say. The kind of job you do most. The neighbourhoods where you work. What a typical assignment involves and why you approach it the way you do. An example of a project with what went wrong and how you solved it.

**How to recognise it:** replace your company name on your website with that of a competitor. Is the text still correct? Then it is too generic.

## Cause 4: nobody else mentions you

A system trusts a company more quickly when it reads the same thing in several places. Your own website is one source, and a biased one. If nothing about you can be found anywhere else, it stops there.

It is not about hundreds of links. It is about a few places that confirm you exist and do what you say: reviews, a listing with a trade association, a supplier that names you as a partner, a piece in the local paper.

**How to recognise it:** search for your company name in quotation marks and skip your own website and social channels. What is left? If the answer is "almost nothing", you know where your work lies.

More on this in [reviews and mentions: why AI trusts others](#art_reviews_en_vermeldingen_waarom_ai_op_anderen_vertrouwt).

## Cause 5: your details contradict each other

Your website says you work across the whole province. Your Google Business Profile only lists Arnhem. An old business directory still has your previous address, and LinkedIn shows a service you stopped offering two years ago.

A person shrugs. A system becomes unsure, and an unsure system chooses the company on which all sources agree.

**How to recognise it:** put your website, your business profile and your LinkedIn page side by side. Are name, address, service area and services the same everywhere?

**What to do:** pick one truth and apply it everywhere. This is an afternoon of work and it is almost always skipped.

## Cause 6: your website cannot be read technically

Sometimes everything is there, and a system still does not see it. That happens in three cases.

- **The text is inside an image.** A handsome banner with your services in it is an empty space to a system.
- **The text only appears after a click or after a script has loaded.** Many systems read the page as it arrives, without waiting or clicking.
- **You are keeping the visitors out yourself.** Some websites block unknown visitors as a precaution, in the security settings or in the file that tells search engines what they may read. In doing so you also shut out the systems you want to reach.

**How to recognise it:** this is hard to see yourself. A simple trial: copy the text of your page by selecting everything and paste it into an empty document. What you see there is roughly what a system reads. If half of it is missing, you have a technical problem.

## Cause 7: nothing happens any more

Your website was delivered three years ago and nothing has changed since. Meanwhile a competitor adds something every month. That has two effects. He has more pages on which a question is answered, and his website shows that the company is alive.

Systems that search live prefer information that is current, certainly for questions about prices, rules and availability. A page about grants with a year from three years ago is not quickly quoted.

**How to recognise it:** what is the date of the last change on your website? If you have to look it up, you know the answer.

## Which cause applies to you?

You do not have to tackle all seven at once. The order below is the order in which we go through them, because the first steps deliver the most for the least work.

1. **Can a system read your website?** If not, the rest is pointless. Start with cause 6.
2. **Does it say what you do, for whom and where?** Cause 1. An hour of work.
3. **Are your details correct everywhere?** Cause 5. An afternoon of work.
4. **Are the answers to your customers' questions on it?** Causes 2 and 3. This is the real work, and it is never finished.
5. **Is your story confirmed by others?** Cause 4. This grows slowly alongside.
6. **Does it stay current?** Cause 7. This is a matter of regularity.

> Most companies are not missing because they do something wrong. They are missing because they never wrote anything down.

## What it is not

A few explanations are doing the rounds that we would like to clear up.

**"You have to pay to be mentioned."** No. At the time of writing you cannot buy a place in the answer of ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude the way you buy an advert. That may change, but for now it is a matter of content.

**"Only large companies are mentioned."** For national questions big names have a head start. For local and specific questions they do not. A system looking for a plumber in Deventer has no use for a national chain without a page about Deventer.

**"It is random."** The answers fluctuate, that is true. But across dozens of questions you keep seeing the same companies, and they have things in common. How to measure that honestly is described in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

**"My website is too old."** Age is not a disadvantage. A website that has existed for years and is updated regularly is better off than a new one. Standing still is the problem, not age.

## An example

A hypothetical example, built from what we often see. Take a landscaping company with twelve staff near Ede. The website has a homepage, a services page, a photo album and a contact form.

To the question "which landscaper in Ede creates a low maintenance garden" a system names three other companies. Why? The photos show beautiful gardens, but there is no text with any of them. The words low maintenance do not appear anywhere on the website. Ede only appears in the address at the bottom.

What is needed fits on one sheet of paper: a page about low maintenance gardens explaining what that means and what it costs, a short description with town and type of garden for five projects, and one sentence about the service area at the top of the homepage. None of that requires a new website.
