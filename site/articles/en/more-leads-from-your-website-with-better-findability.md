More leads from your website with better findability: it sounds like a sum. More visitors, so more enquiries. In practice that sum rarely adds up.

We see websites with thousands of visitors per month and two enquiries. And we see websites with two hundred visitors and ten enquiries. The difference is not in the quantity. It is in who comes, on which question, and what they find.

In this article we walk the whole route. From the question someone asks to the moment they get in touch.

## Why more visitors does not produce more leads

Suppose you run a painting company and write an article about the history of paint. It is read widely. Pupils use it for a school project. Not a single customer comes out of it.

If you write a page about what exterior painting costs for a terraced house instead, perhaps thirty people a month come. All of them have a house that needs painting.

That is the difference between reach and findability at the right moment. A lead arises when three things coincide:

1. Someone has a problem you solve.
2. They ask a question about it.
3. You are the answer they find.

Most websites can only be found for their own name. That brings in people who already know you. Those are not new leads.

## Which questions you want to be found for

Not every question is worth the same. It helps to sort them by how close someone is to a decision.

### Finding their bearings

"What is the difference between a heat pump and a hybrid boiler?" This person is learning. They will buy in six months, or never. These questions produce a lot of visits and few enquiries. They are useful for becoming known.

### Comparing

"What does a hybrid heat pump cost including installation?" "What should I look for when choosing an installer?" This person has decided they want something and is working out how. This is the most underrated group. Whoever gives an honest and concrete answer here is the first to be called.

### Choosing

"Which installer in Zwolle fits hybrid heat pumps?" This is the question just before the enquiry. This is where you want to be mentioned.

Most business owners, if they write at all, write about finding your bearings. The leads are in comparing and choosing. How to uncover your customers' questions is covered in [what questions do customers ask AI](#art_welke_vragen_stellen_klanten_aan_ai).

## What changes now that people use AI

In the past someone made their choice on your website. They clicked on five companies, compared and called two.

Now that comparing happens in the conversation with an AI assistant more and more often. They ask what it costs, what to look out for and who does it well. When they then arrive at your website, they already know a lot.

That has two consequences for your leads.

**You get fewer visitors who know nothing yet, and more visitors who have nearly decided.** The number of visitors can fall while the number of enquiries stays level or rises. So look at enquiries and not only at visits.

**Your page has to confirm what they have already heard.** If the AI assistant said you specialise in older homes and they see nothing of that on your website, they drop out. The description in the answer and the content of your page have to match.

## The page they land on

Findability brings someone to your page. After that the page has to do the work. These are the six things we keep seeing on pages that perform well.

### 1. They see at once that they are in the right place

The heading and the first sentence confirm the question they came with. If they were looking for a painter for exterior work in Deventer, they read that you do exterior painting in Deventer and the surrounding area. Not "welcome to our website".

### 2. There is a price, or at least a direction

This is the point where most business owners back off. "Every job is different." That is true, and the customer knows it too. They do not want a quote. They want to know whether to think of a thousand or ten thousand euros.

Give a range and explain what it depends on. Whoever does that gets fewer enquiries from people for whom it is too expensive, and more from people who know what they are getting into. Besides, it is exactly the kind of information an AI assistant adopts.

### 3. They read how it goes

What happens after they get in touch? Do you come round? How long before there is a quote? How long does the job take? Uncertainty about the process is one of the biggest reasons not to call.

### 4. They see proof

Two or three projects that resemble theirs. With the town, the type of work and what was special about it. A review from a customer with a name and a town does more than ten without.

### 5. There is one clear next step

Not five buttons. One. "Request a quote" or "Call for an appointment". And make that step small. A form with three fields is completed more often than one with twelve.

### 6. They can do it on their phone

Most people search on a phone. A phone number you can tap and a form that works on a small screen are not luxuries.

## Also say what you are not for

This is one of the most valuable things we have learned, and it feels contrary.

A client of ours runs a mobile scooter repair service. He received many enquiries from regions he does not serve. Each enquiry cost him time: reading, replying, explaining that he does not travel there. Since his website clearly states where he does and does not work, he gets more enquiries from his own region and fewer from outside it.

So fewer enquiries can be an improvement. A lead you cannot serve is not a lead.

Write on your website:

- in which towns you work, and where you do not;
- which kind of jobs you take on, and which not;
- from which amount or size it becomes worthwhile for you.

An AI assistant adopts that. "This company only works in the Twente region" is useful information to a system. It makes sure you are mentioned for the right question. More on this in [getting found locally in AI](#art_lokaal_gevonden_worden_in_ai).

## A worked example

A fictional example to show the principle. The numbers are made up.

A roofer has a website with a homepage and a services page. He gets four hundred visitors per month, nearly all on his company name. Four enquiries come out of that.

Over three months he adds six pages. What a new flat roof costs. How long roofing lasts. A leak at a roof window, what now. Insulating a roof from the inside or the outside. Which grants exist. And a page about his service area.

Visits rise to six hundred. That is not spectacular. But the two hundred new visitors all have a roof that needs something done. The number of enquiries goes from four to ten.

The gain is not in the six hundred. It is in who those two hundred are.

## How to measure whether it works

Many business owners do not know where their enquiries come from. Then you cannot see what works either. Three simple habits help.

1. **Ask.** Put the question "How did you find us?" in your form and ask it on the phone too. You will hear "through ChatGPT" more and more often. You will not see that reliably in any analytics program.
2. **Count enquiries per month, not visitors.** Keep a list. Date, type of question, how they found you, job or no job.
3. **See which pages produce enquiries.** In most analytics programs you can see on which page someone arrived before completing the form. Those are the pages you want more of.

After three months you see a pattern. Two or three pages produce most of your enquiries. That is where your next step lies: more pages of that kind.

## The order in which to tackle it

Whoever wants everything at once does nothing. This is an order that works.

1. **First improve the page that already gets visits.** Usually your homepage and your main service. Price indication, process, proof, one button. This delivers something straight away, without your findability changing.
2. **Make your service area and your limits clear.** An hour of work, and your wrong enquiries decrease.
3. **Write three pages about comparison questions.** Cost, duration and what to look out for.
4. **Measure where you are mentioned.** Test your most important questions in the AI systems, as described in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).
5. **Carry on.** Two or three pages more each month, based on what you measure.

You can do steps 1 and 2 this week. Steps 3 to 5 are the work that is never finished.

## What not to expect

Better findability is not a tap you turn on. It takes weeks before new pages are picked up, and months before you see a stable effect. If you need customers next week, you are better off with adverts.

The advantage is on the other side. An advert stops when you stop paying. A good page keeps producing enquiries for years. The two do not exclude each other either.

And findability does not fix a poor offer. If your price is off or you never call anyone back, no page will help.

## Three small changes with a large effect

Besides the six points above, there are three details that are quick to fix and that we see overlooked again and again.

**Reply faster than the others.** A customer who got three names from an AI assistant contacts all three. The company that answers within the hour often gets the job. State on your contact page how fast you reply, and keep to it.

**Repeat the reassurance next to the button.** "No obligation", "we call back within one working day" or "the site visit is free". The moment of clicking is the moment of doubt. One short line next to the button removes it.

**Use the customer's words in the heading.** If customers call it a "leaking flat roof", the heading should not say "bitumen restoration". The customer has to recognise their own problem in the first second, and a system looks for the same match.
