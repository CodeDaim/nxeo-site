"Is my company mentioned in AI?" It is the question we hear most, and nearly everyone answers it the same way. They open ChatGPT, type in their company name and read a tidy paragraph about themselves. Conclusion: we are in there.

Unfortunately that is not a test. If you ask a system about a name, it looks that name up and summarises your own website. That tells you nothing about what happens when someone who does not know you yet asks for a recommendation. And that is exactly the moment you win or lose a customer.

This article describes the test we run ourselves, in a form you can carry out in half an hour. No tools and no account with us required.

## Why typing in your own name proves nothing

There are two kinds of questions people ask AI about companies.

The first kind is a **name question**. "What does Baker Installations do?" Whoever asks that already knows you. The system looks you up and reports what it finds. You are almost always mentioned, because your name is in the question.

The second kind is a **choice question**. "Which installer in Zwolle is good with heat pumps?" Whoever asks that does not know you yet. The system has to pick a few companies itself. This is the question that matters.

The difference is large. We regularly see companies that get a neat answer to every name question and do not appear in a single choice question. They believe they are visible. Their new customers see someone else.

## Step 1: write down ten questions customers really ask

Take a sheet of paper and write down ten questions. Not the keywords you would like to be found for, but whole sentences the way a customer would type or speak them.

A good list has three kinds of questions:

1. **Recommendations.** "Who is a good bookkeeper for freelancers in Amersfoort?" Four of your ten questions belong here.
2. **Comparisons and costs.** "What does it cost to fit a dormer and what should I look for when choosing a company?" People ask this before they call anyone.
3. **Problems.** "My boiler keeps losing pressure, who near Deventer can fix that quickly?" Here the customer is closest to an enquiry.

Unsure about the wording? Think of the last five phone calls from new customers. How did they start? Those are your questions. We go deeper into this in [what questions do customers ask AI](#art_welke_vragen_stellen_klanten_aan_ai).

Important: do not put your own company name in any of the questions. Do name your town or region if you work locally, because a customer does that too.

## Step 2: ask them in three systems, in a clean conversation

Ask each question in at least three systems. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are a good start, and if you have time to spare add Perplexity. They get their information from different places, so the answers diverge. A company that leads in ChatGPT can be absent in Gemini. You can read why in [Gemini does not mention my company](#art_gemini_noemt_mijn_bedrijf_niet) and in [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden).

Three rules keep your test honest:

- **Start a new conversation for every question.** A system remembers what you asked earlier in the same conversation. If you have just mentioned your own name, that colours the next answer.
- **Switch off memory and personal settings**, or use a window in which you are not logged in. Otherwise you get an answer tuned to you, and not the answer your customer gets.
- **Do not change the question halfway.** Ask the exact same sentence in each system. Otherwise you are comparing apples and oranges.

## Step 3: note four things per answer

Make a simple table. Ten rows for your questions, three columns for the systems. In each cell you note four things:

1. **Are you mentioned?** Yes or no.
2. **In which place?** First, somewhere in the middle or last in a row.
3. **Who else is mentioned?** Write down the names. This is the list of companies you compete with in the eyes of AI, and it is often different from what you expect.
4. **Is what is said about you correct?** Sometimes you are mentioned with a service you no longer offer, or in a town where you do not work.

That fourth point is often skipped, although it yields a lot. A system that describes you wrongly sends the wrong customers your way. What to do about that is covered in [AI says something wrong about my company](#art_ai_zegt_iets_verkeerds_over_mijn_bedrijf).

## Step 4: repeat it, because one answer is chance

This is the part most people do not know. An AI system does not give the same answer to the same question every time. Ask the same question five minutes later and there may be a different set of companies.

That is not a fault. It is how these systems work. But it does mean that being mentioned once says little, and being absent once says little too.

So repeat your ten questions on another day. If you appear both times, you stand firm. If you appear one of the two times, you are on the edge. If you never appear, you do not exist for that question.

> It is not about being mentioned once. It is about how often, across all the questions your customers ask.

## How to read the outcome

After two rounds, count the cells in which you are mentioned. You have sixty: ten questions, three systems, two rounds.

- **Mentioned almost nowhere.** This is the most common outcome, so do not be alarmed. It usually means there is too little on your website that a system can work with. The causes are listed in [why am I not in AI recommendations](#art_waarom_sta_ik_niet_in_de_aanbevelingen_van_ai).
- **In one system but not in another.** Then one system finds you and the other does not. That often has a technical cause, or you are stronger in one search index than in another.
- **For problems but not for recommendations.** You have good explanatory pages, but nowhere does it clearly say what you do, for whom and where.
- **Mentioned everywhere, but described wrongly.** Then there is work to do on your details: conflicting information on your website, your business profile and elsewhere.

Next, look at the names that come back most often. Open the website of number one and read it as a customer. What is there that is missing from yours? Nine times out of ten the answer is not complicated: they explain things, you list them.

## The three mistakes we see most

**Testing too kindly.** People ask questions they know will turn out well. "Which company in Zwolle specialises in listed window frames with double glazing from before 1900?" If you make the question so narrow that only you remain, you measure nothing. Ask the question the way the customer asks it, even if that is a broad question where you are up against big names.

**Testing once and leaving it there.** Answers shift. A competitor publishes something, a system gets a new version, a search index is refreshed. A measurement from three months ago says little about today.

**Only looking at yourself.** The value lies in the comparison. That you are mentioned for three of the ten questions only becomes interesting when you know your competitor appears for eight of the ten. How to approach that is covered in [your competitor is mentioned in ChatGPT](#art_je_concurrent_wordt_wel_genoemd_in_chatgpt).

## How we do it

The test above is a small version of what we run every day. The principle is the same, the scale is different.

For a client we first draw up a list of the questions their customers ask. We put that list to the systems again every day, so we do not depend on one accidental answer. We count how often the company is mentioned, in which place and whether the description is correct. We ask the same questions for the competitors, so you can see where they win and you do not.

That produces one number, the Presence score, and above all a list of questions you are currently losing. That list decides what gets written next.

You can do this by hand. Half an hour a week is enough to get a feel for it. It only becomes hard to keep it up and to draw conclusions when the answers fluctuate. That is the reason we automated it.

## What to do with the outcome

A test is only worth something if you act on it. Start small.

1. **Pick the three questions where you would most like to be mentioned** and where you are currently missing.
2. **Check whether the answer to that question is on your website.** Not between the lines, but literally. Usually it is not there.
3. **Write one page or one clear block per question.** The question as the heading, the answer in the first two sentences, the explanation below.
4. **Test again in four to six weeks.** Systems need time to pick up new content. How long exactly is covered in [how long until AI mentions your company](#art_hoe_lang_duurt_het_voordat_ai_je_bedrijf_noemt).

## Frequently asked questions about this test

### Do I need a paid account?

No. The free versions of ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are enough, and those are also the versions most of your customers use. Do make sure the system is allowed to search the internet. In most of them that is on by default.

### Does it matter where I am when I test?

Sometimes. Systems can use your location for questions like "near me". So always put the town in your question. Then you test what you mean to test and you do not depend on where your laptop happens to be.

### I am not mentioned anywhere. Is that bad?

Above all it is normal. Most small and medium sized companies are not yet mentioned for choice questions, simply because their website was not built for that. The good news is that your competitors are in the same position. Whoever starts now is ahead.

### How often should I repeat this?

By hand, once a month is a reasonable rhythm. More often makes little sense if nothing has changed on your website in the meantime.
