Type a question into Google and there is a good chance you first see a block of text. A summary of a few paragraphs, with a handful of sources to the right or beneath it. Only below that do the regular results begin.

That block is called an AI Overview. Google has shown them in the Netherlands since May 2025. For many business owners the question since then is no longer only how to rank higher, but how to go about getting into Google AI Overviews.

The good news is that it is not a secret art. Google explains fairly openly how it works. In this article we set that out and translate it into what you can do on your own website.

## What an AI Overview is

An AI Overview is an answer Google writes itself, based on pages from its search results. It appears above or between the regular results. With the answer there are links to the pages that were used.

Three things are good to know.

**It does not always appear.** Google only shows an overview when it thinks it adds something. For a search for a brand or an address you usually see none. For a question that calls for explanation you do.

**It is slightly different every time.** The answer is built again. The sources can change.

**It is not the same as AI Mode.** AI Mode is a separate tab in Google in which you hold a conversation. It came to the Netherlands in October 2025. The technology is related and what is written here largely applies to both.

## How Google chooses the sources

This is the core, and it is different from what many people think.

You would expect Google to take the top three results and summarise them. It does not. Google describes in its [guidance for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) that for an AI answer it runs several searches at once, on subtopics of the question. It calls that fanning out the query.

An example. Someone searches: "is a heat pump suitable for a house from the seventies?" Google can split that into questions about insulation, about the type of heating in older houses, about costs and about grants. For each of those parts it looks for pages. The answer is put together from those pages.

The consequence matters for smaller companies:

> You do not have to rank first for the main question. You have to be the best answer to one part of it.

Google itself writes that as a result a wider and more varied set of links comes into view than with a regular search. An installer with one very good page about heat pumps in poorly insulated houses can appear as a source alongside large national websites.

## The requirements: fewer than you think

Google is unusually clear here. There are no additional requirements to appear in an AI Overview. What is required:

- **Your page is indexed by Google.** What Google does not know, it cannot use.
- **Your page may be shown with a snippet.** Some websites have settings that restrict that. Then you drop out here too.

That is all that is mandatory. You do not need a special file, separate markup for AI or new technology. Google also says honestly that it gives no guarantee a page will be used.

You check whether your pages are known to Google by typing the word site, a colon and your domain name into Google. In Search Console you can see the status per page.

## What raises the odds

Meeting the requirements is one thing. Being chosen is another. These are the things that make the difference in our experience.

### Put the answer at the top

An AI Overview consists of short, firm sentences. Google looks in pages for passages it can adopt or summarise in that form. A page that begins with the answer makes that easy.

Compare these two openings of a page about the cost of a fence.

The first: "A fence is an important investment in your garden. At our company we believe it is important that you are well informed. In this article we look at the many factors that play a part."

The second: "Having a wooden fence fitted costs on average between 90 and 160 euros per metre, including materials and labour. The price mainly depends on the type of wood, the height and the ground."

The amounts in that example are made up, but the principle is clear. The second opening is an answer. The first is a run up.

### Give each part of the question its own heading

Because Google searches on parts of a question, it helps when those parts are visibly on your page. As a heading, in the customer's words.

A page about roof insulation with the headings "What does roof insulation cost?", "Insulating from the inside or the outside?", "How much do you save?" and "Which grant is available?" answers four parts. Each heading with the paragraph beneath it is a separate block Google can use.

### Write what only you know

This is where generic texts lose out. If ten pages say the same thing, Google has no reason to choose yours. The page that adds something stands out.

What do you add? Experience. What you come across in practice. An installer can write that in houses from the seventies the pipes are often too thin for a fully electric heat pump, and what he advises in that case. That is not on a comparison site.

### Keep it current

For topics where amounts and rules change, Google looks at how recent the information is. A page about grants with the scheme from two years ago is not quickly chosen. Put a date with such information and update it when something changes.

### Put everything in text

Google states it explicitly: important content belongs in text. A price table as an image does not count. Information that only appears when you click on something is read less reliably.

### Keep your business profile current

For local questions Google also uses the details from business profiles. Google names keeping them current in the same list of recommendations. More on that in [getting found locally in AI](#art_lokaal_gevonden_worden_in_ai).

## And structured data?

You often hear that you must add special markup so that Google understands your page better. It is more nuanced than that.

Google says you do not need new or special markup for the AI answers. Structured data does remain useful for the regular results, and it helps Google understand what is on a page. The condition Google sets is that it matches what is visibly on the page.

Our practical conclusion: it is a good addition to a good page. It does not rescue a poor page.

## Will I lose visitors because of AI Overviews?

For some searches, yes. If the answer is complete at the top, not everyone clicks through.

Two things put that in perspective.

First, it is mainly the simple questions where clicking through declines. Questions followed by a decision work differently. Someone looking for a builder reads the overview and then looks at the companies in it.

Second, being named as a source is worth more than a regular listing lower on the page. You are at the top, next to the answer, as the party that knows.

The traffic that comes from AI answers counts in Search Console. You see it in the regular performance report, not as a separate category. How to turn visits into enquiries is covered in [more leads from your website with better findability](#art_meer_leads_uit_je_website_met_betere_vindbaarheid).

## Can I choose not to be in them?

Yes. Google offers settings with which you can limit which text from your page is shown in the results. Those also apply to the AI Overviews. For most companies that is not a good idea, because you also limit your regular listing.

We mention it because it is sometimes switched on by accident. On websites taken over from another administrator we come across settings nobody can explain any more. If you are missing everywhere, have this checked.

## How to test whether you are in them

1. **Write down ten questions** customers ask about your trade. Use whole sentences.
2. **Search for them in Google**, in a window in which you are not logged in.
3. **Note per question** whether an AI Overview appears and which sources are listed.
4. **Open the sources.** See how those pages are built. You will spot the pattern quickly: short answers, clear headings, concrete information.
5. **Repeat it after a month.** The sources change. Whoever is named twice stands firm.

If no overview appears for any of your questions, your questions are probably too short or too commercial. Try questions that start with how, what or why.

## An example

An invented example, built from what we often see. A window frame company has a page "uPVC window frames" with product photos and a button for a quote. That page ranks reasonably in the regular results and is not used in a single AI Overview.

The company adds a page titled "uPVC or wooden frames: what suits a house from the thirties?" The page opens with an answer in two sentences. Four headings follow: maintenance, appearance, insulation and cost. Under each heading is what the company comes across in such houses.

That page is not number one anywhere for a keyword. It is the best answer to the part of the question about appearance in older houses. That is exactly the kind of page that ends up in an overview.

## Common misunderstandings

**"I need to rank in the top three first."** No. Because Google searches on parts of the question, sources regularly come from lower positions, as long as the page answers one part well.

**"Longer pages are chosen more often."** Length is not the criterion. A page of four hundred words that answers one part completely does better than three thousand words in which the answer is hard to find.

**"Once I am in, I stay in."** The overview is rebuilt and the sources rotate. Being in it is not a possession. It is something you keep earning with pages that stay current.

**"It only matters for informational websites."** Companies that sell services benefit just as much. The questions just before a purchase, about cost, duration and suitability, are exactly the ones for which overviews appear.

## In summary

- An AI Overview is an answer from Google with links to the pages used.
- Google splits questions into parts and looks for pages for each of them.
- There are no separate requirements. Your page has to be in Google and be allowed to be shown.
- The odds rise when the answer is at the top, each part has a heading and you add something from your own experience.
