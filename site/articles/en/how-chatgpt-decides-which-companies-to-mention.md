Someone types into ChatGPT: "Which installer near Zwolle is good with heat pumps?" A few seconds later there is an answer with three names. No list of ten blue links, no adverts, just three companies with a short explanation next to each.

Are you one of the three? And if not, why is your competitor?

That is the question more and more business owners are asking. In this article we explain how ChatGPT arrives at an answer like that, which signals count and what you can do to **get mentioned in ChatGPT**. Without jargon.

## AI answers are not search results

With Google you are used to a ranking. Your page sits in position three, seven or twenty two, and with enough work you move up. That ranking is largely the same for everyone.

ChatGPT works differently. There is no fixed list. The system writes a new answer for each question, and that answer depends on how the question is phrased, what the user asked before and which sources the system consults at that moment. Two people with nearly the same question can get different answers.

That has two consequences:

- **There is no position one to claim.** You are mentioned or you are not, and that changes per question.
- **You cannot check it with one search.** If you type in your own company name once and see yourself, you have learned nothing.

That is why we do not look at a single position. We look at how often a company is mentioned across a whole list of questions that customers really ask. You can read more about that in the [frequently asked questions](#faq).

## Where ChatGPT gets its knowledge

To understand why one company is mentioned and another is not, you need to know that an AI system draws on two sources.

### What the model already knows

A language model is trained on an enormous amount of text from the internet. Anything that was written about a company often and clearly before that training can end up in the model. Think of your own website, mentions on other sites, articles, reviews and business directories.

A company that has been mentioned in many places for years has a head start here. A company that has left hardly any traces online simply does not exist for the model.

### What the system looks up in the moment

On top of that, ChatGPT and similar systems can search the web live. That happens often with questions about local companies, current prices or "the best in the area". The system looks up pages, reads them and summarises what it finds.

That is good news. It means you do not have to wait for a new model to be trained. What you put on your website today can count in an answer within a few weeks.

## The signals that count

Nobody outside the makers of these systems knows exactly how they work, and the workings keep changing. What we can do is test: which companies are mentioned, and what do they have in common? In our own research and client work the same things keep coming back.

### 1. There is a clear answer on your own website

This is the most important one. An AI system looks for text that answers a question. If someone asks which installer fits heat pumps in Zwolle, the system needs a page that literally says you fit heat pumps, in Zwolle and the surrounding area, and what is involved.

Many websites do not say that. They say "we deliver custom work" and "quality comes first", but not what you do, for whom and where. For a person that is already vague. For an AI system it is unusable.

### 2. You write about the questions customers ask

People ask ChatGPT whole questions, not loose keywords. "What does a heat pump cost for a terraced house?" "Can I get a heat pump if I do not have underfloor heating?" Companies that answer exactly those questions on their website are used as a source more often.

It does not need to be a long article. A page with frequently asked questions, a short explanation per service or an article on one topic all work. What matters is that the question and the answer are there.

### 3. Others mention you too

A system trusts a company more quickly if it reads the same thing in several places. Reviews, a listing with a trade association, an interview in a local paper, a supplier that names you as a partner: it all counts. Not because it is a trick, but because it confirms the same story.

### 4. Your details match everywhere

Name, address, service area and what you do need to match on your website, in your Google Business Profile and in other places. Conflicting information makes a system unsure, and an unsure system prefers to mention someone else.

### 5. Your website is technically readable

An AI system reads the raw text of your page. Text that only exists inside an image, or that only appears after a click that loads extra data, is often missed. A clear structure with headings helps. Structured data helps too, such as the markup for frequently asked questions that [schema.org describes](https://schema.org/FAQPage). With it you tell a system in its own language what the question is and what the answer is.

## Why most companies are missing

Reading the above, you can see the pattern. Most websites of small and medium sized companies were built as a business card. They look good, but there is very little in them.

The three reasons we come across most:

1. **Too little content.** Five pages with three sentences each. There is nothing for a system to quote.
2. **Too generic.** The text could sit on any competitor's website. Nothing that sets you apart or makes your service area clear.
3. **Never updated.** The website was delivered three years ago and has not been touched since. Meanwhile a competitor publishes something new every month.

The good news is that all three can be fixed. You do not need a bigger marketing budget than your competitor. You need regularity.

## An example from our own practice

We noticed ourselves how fast it can go. On our own website we published a few articles about carpenters: what a good website for a carpentry business needs and which questions their customers ask.

Five to six weeks later two enquiries came in within one week. Both from a carpenter, both from the same region. In the eight years before that, this had never happened out of the blue.

> A few articles about one topic were enough to be found by exactly the people we wrote about.

That is no guarantee it will go this way for every company. It does show what happens when you are clear about one topic for one audience.

## It works the other way round too

Being mentioned is not always the goal. Sometimes you want to be mentioned less in places where you have no business.

A client of ours, a mobile scooter repair service, received many enquiries from regions he does not serve at all. That cost him time and delivered nothing. Since it became clear where he does and does not work, he is approached more often from his own region and less from outside it.

For an AI system, "we do not work here" is just as valuable as "we do work here". The clearer you are, the better the answer fits what you want.

## What you can do this week

You do not have to wait to get started. These are four things you can do yourself.

1. **Ask the question yourself.** Open ChatGPT and ask five questions your customers would ask, without naming your own company. Write down who is mentioned.
2. **Read your homepage as a stranger would.** Do the first three sentences say what you do, for whom and where? If not, start there.
3. **Write down ten questions customers often ask you.** On the phone, by email or at the first appointment. Each of those questions deserves a page or a paragraph.
4. **Check your details.** Do your name, address and service area match on your website and in your Google Business Profile?

## Can you guarantee a mention?

No, and anyone who promises that is promising too much. Which company an AI system mentions also depends on how strong your competitors are, how well known you already are and how the systems themselves change. Nobody has influence over part of that.

What you do control is whether there is something to find. A company with clear, current content about its customers' questions has a far greater chance than a company whose website is a business card. And that gap widens as more people put their questions to AI instead of a search engine.

## In summary

- ChatGPT does not keep a ranking. It writes a new answer for every question.
- It draws on what the model already knows and on what it finds online at that moment.
- The biggest factor in your own hands is clear content on your own website about your customers' questions.
- Mentions by others and matching details reinforce that.
- You cannot force it, but you can raise the odds considerably.
