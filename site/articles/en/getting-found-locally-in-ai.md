"Which plumber in Deventer can still come today?" For a local company that is the question everything turns on. Not how you rank nationally, but whether you are mentioned in your own town.

Getting found locally in AI has its own rules. An AI assistant does not know where your van drives. It only knows what can be read about it. And on most websites there is surprisingly little to read about that.

In this article we explain how a system determines where you work, and how to make that clear without resorting to tricks.

## How an AI system knows where you work

A person sees your address in Deventer and understands that you also go to Twello and Olst. A system does not simply draw that conclusion. It looks for confirmation in three kinds of sources.

**Your own website.** Does it say in text where you work? Not just an address, but a sentence like "we work in Deventer, Twello, Olst and Bathmen"?

**Your business profile and other listings.** In your Google Business Profile you can fill in a service area. Business directories and trade associations often state a region. Gemini and the AI answers in Google lean on this heavily.

**What others write about you.** A review that says "helped quickly in Twello" is confirmation to a system that you go to Twello.

The more of these sources say the same thing, the more certain a system is. And only when it is certain does it mention you for a town.

## The problem with only an address

Most local companies have their town in exactly one place: in the address at the bottom of the page. Two things go wrong there.

First, an address says where you are based, not where you work. A landscaper with a yard in Epe may mainly work in Apeldoorn. A system that only sees the address links him to Epe.

Second, an address is not an answer to a question. When someone asks who builds gardens in Apeldoorn, a system looks for a page that says someone builds gardens in Apeldoorn. A postcode in the footer is not that.

## Step 1: write out your service area

Start on your homepage. Put a sentence in the first paragraph with what you do and where. "We build and maintain gardens in Apeldoorn, Epe, Vaassen and Ugchelen."

In addition, create a separate page about your service area. It states:

- **The towns where you work,** by name. Not only "the Veluwe region", because people ask about their own town.
- **How far you travel.** "Up to about thirty kilometres around Apeldoorn."
- **Whether there are travel costs** and from where.
- **Where you do not go.** More on that below.

That is an hour of work, and for many companies the biggest step.

## Step 2: complete your business profile

For local questions your Google Business Profile is one of the most important sources. It decides whether you appear on the map, and Google names keeping it current in its recommendations for the AI answers.

Go through these points:

1. **Category.** Choose the category that best describes what you do. "Roofer" and not "contractor" if you only do roofs.
2. **Service area.** You can enter towns and regions. Fill them in.
3. **Services.** Put them all in, with a short description.
4. **Opening hours.** Including different hours around public holidays.
5. **Photos.** Of recent work, with in the description what it is and where.
6. **Reviews.** Respond to every review. In your reply you can name the town and the type of work.

Then check that everything matches your website. A different service area in your profile than on your website makes a system unsure.

## Step 3: show your work per town

This is the part that works most strongly and is done least.

Every completed job is proof that you work somewhere. A short description of a project, with the town and the type of work, tells a system more than any claim.

It does not have to be much. Five sentences per project:

- Where was it? Town, possibly the neighbourhood.
- What was the customer's question?
- What did you do?
- Was there anything special about it?
- How long did it take?

"In the Zevenhuizen neighbourhood in Apeldoorn we replaced the gutters and the fascia on a corner house from 1982. The old zinc gutters leaked at the seams. We replaced them in two days." That answers several questions at once. Who does gutters in Apeldoorn. What is often wrong with gutters on houses from that period. How long it takes.

Ten descriptions like that, spread across your service area, do more than ten pages of general text.

## Step 4: make a page per region, but do it properly

There is an old habit of making a page for every town with the same text. "Plumber Deventer", "Plumber Twello", "Plumber Olst". Only the place name differs.

That no longer works, and it never worked well. Search engines recognise the pattern. An AI system finds nothing on such a page that it did not already know.

A page per town or region can work if it contains something that applies only to that town.

**What you come across in that town.** In the centre of Deventer there are many old buildings with lead pipes. In the new housing estate something else is at play.

**Projects you have done there.** With a short description.

**Practical matters.** How quickly you can be there. Whether parking is a problem. Whether there are council rules that matter.

**Reviews from customers in that town.**

If you have nothing of your own to say about a town, do not make a page for it. Name it on your service area page. Three good regional pages are better than twenty empty ones.

## Step 5: say where you do not go

This feels like sending customers away. It works the other way round.

A client of ours runs a mobile scooter repair service. He received many enquiries from towns where he does not travel. Every enquiry cost time and delivered nothing. Since his website clearly states where he does and does not work, he is approached more often from his own region and less from outside it.

For an AI system, "we do not work here" is just as useful as "we do work here". It helps to mention you for the right question and not for the wrong one.

Simply write it down. "We do not work west of the IJssel." "For jobs in Zwolle we will gladly refer you on." It does not cost you customers. It only costs you the phone calls you could do nothing with.

## Step 6: make sure others name your town

Your own website is one source. A system looks for confirmation.

**Reviews with a town in them.** Do not only ask customers for a rating, but to describe what you did and where. "Gutter replaced in Twello, tidy and fast" is worth more than five stars without text.

**Local mentions.** The business association, the sports club you sponsor, a supplier that lists its dealers per region, the local paper. They are not big names, but they tie you to a place.

**Matching details.** The same name, the same address and the same phone number everywhere. Differences make a system unsure whether it is dealing with the same company.

More on this in [reviews and mentions: why AI trusts others](#art_reviews_en_vermeldingen_waarom_ai_op_anderen_vertrouwt).

## How to test it

1. **Ask the question with your town.** "Which roofer in Apeldoorn is good with flat roofs?" Do that in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.
2. **Repeat it for each town in your service area.** You will see that you are mentioned in one town and not in another.
3. **Ask the system where you work.** "In which towns does this company work?" with your name added. The answer shows what a system thinks it knows about your service area. If that differs from reality, you know what is missing from your website.
4. **Search Google Maps for your service and town.** Are you in the first results? If not, start with your business profile.

The full test method is in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

## Differences between the systems

Not every system handles local questions in the same way.

**Gemini and Google** have the advantage of Google Maps and the business profiles. For local questions they are often the most precise, and the most dependent on your profile. See [Gemini does not mention my company](#art_gemini_noemt_mijn_bedrijf_niet).

**ChatGPT and Claude** mainly have to rely on what they find on websites. Here your own text weighs more heavily. A company with an empty profile and an excellent website can come out well here, and vice versa.

That is the reason to do both. Your profile for Google's systems, your website for the rest.

## An example

A made up example, put together from what we often see. A painting company is based in Rijssen and works across the whole of Twente. The website shows the address in Rijssen and the sentence "active in the region".

For the question about a painter in Rijssen the company is sometimes mentioned. For Nijverdal, Holten and Almelo never. The word Almelo is nowhere on the website.

What is needed: a service area page with the towns by name, the service area in the business profile, and eight project descriptions from different towns. After that, the request to every new customer to mention in their review where the work was.

None of that is complicated. It has simply never been written down.

## Common mistakes

**Claiming too large an area.** "We work throughout the Netherlands" while in practice you stay within forty kilometres. You are then not convincingly mentioned for any town.

**Only naming the region.** "The Achterhoek" is vaguer to a system than Doetinchem, Winterswijk and Zelhem. Name both.

**Listing place names without a sentence.** A row of forty place names at the bottom of the page reads like a trick and is treated as one. Put them in sentences and with projects.

**Forgetting the business profile after a move.** It happens more often than you think.

**No town in the reviews.** Fifty ratings of five stars without text say nothing about where you work.

## What about companies without a fixed area?

Not every company works within a ring around its premises. Three situations call for a slightly different approach.

**You work at the customer's site across a wide area.** Name the regions you serve regularly and say honestly where you only come for larger jobs. "For projects from a certain size we travel across the whole province" tells a customer and a system what to expect.

**You have several branches.** Give each branch its own page with its own address, opening hours, team and projects, and its own business profile. One shared page for all branches gives a system nothing to connect to a particular town.

**You work online and locally.** State clearly which services you deliver remotely and which only in your region. Otherwise a system will tie everything to your address, or to nothing at all.

In all three cases the principle stays the same: write down in ordinary sentences what is true, and make sure the same thing can be read elsewhere.

## In summary

- A system mentions you for a town when several sources confirm that you work there.
- Write out your service area in sentences, with towns by name.
- Keep your business profile complete and the same as your website.
- Show projects per town. That is the strongest proof.
- Only make regional pages if you have something of your own to say.
- Also say where you do not go.
