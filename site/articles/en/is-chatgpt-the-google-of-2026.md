"ChatGPT, the Google of 2026?" It is a headline that sounds good and that you come across everywhere this year. It is half true. The other half matters more to anyone who runs a company.

Let us start with what is really happening, and then look at what you should do with it.

## What the numbers say

In September 2025 OpenAI published a [study of how people use ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/). At that point ChatGPT had around 700 million users per week. Some 70 percent of consumer use had nothing to do with work. And roughly half of all messages fell into the category of asking: people looking for information or advice.

That last part is the interesting one. ChatGPT began as a tool for writing texts. To a large extent it has become a place where people look things up.

It has grown since then. This autumn a product lead at ChatGPT said in an interview that the number of searches is around a fifth of what Google handles, with more than a billion users per week. That is the company's own statement and not an independent measurement, so take the figure with a pinch of salt. The direction is clear enough.

## Why the answer is still no

A fifth of Google is a lot. It also means four fifths still happens at Google. And Google is not standing still. Since 2025 it has shown AI Overviews above the results in the Netherlands too, and it has its own AI Mode.

More important is that people are not switching. They use both. Anyone who quickly needs an address, opening hours or an online shop types that into Google. Anyone with a question that needs explaining puts it to an AI assistant more and more often.

So ChatGPT is not the new Google. It is a second place where people search, with different rules.

## The real difference: from list to advice

Here is the half that counts for business owners. The difference between Google and ChatGPT is not the number of users. It is the shape of the answer.

**Google gives a list.** Ten results, a map, some adverts. The user chooses. If you are in position four, you still have a reasonable chance that someone clicks on you.

**ChatGPT gives advice.** Two or three names, with a short explanation of why. The user no longer has to choose. The choice has already been made.

That changes three things.

### There is no position four any more

In a list of ten, everyone takes part a little. In advice with three names, seven companies do not take part. The gap between being mentioned and not being mentioned is larger than the gap between position three and position seven ever was.

### The customer arrives better prepared

Someone who reaches you through an AI assistant has already asked what it costs, what to look out for and who does it well. They are not calling to find their bearings. They are calling to make an appointment. For whoever is mentioned, that is pleasant. The conversation goes faster and the enquiry is more serious.

### You do not see it happen

In Google you can measure how often you are shown and how often someone clicked. With an AI assistant you see nothing. A customer asks a question, gets three names and calls one of the three. You do not know the conversation took place.

That is why many business owners think it does not affect them. They see no decline, only growth that fails to arrive. What it means to be left out is worked out in [if AI does not mention your company, you do not exist](#art_als_ai_jouw_bedrijf_niet_noemt_besta_je_niet).

## Which questions go to AI and which stay with Google?

There is no hard line, but there is a pattern we keep seeing.

**Mostly stays with Google:**

- Short searches for a known company or brand.
- Practical matters: address, route, opening hours, phone number.
- Comparing products on price and buying straight away.
- Anything you want to see on a map.

**Shifts to AI:**

- Questions that involve weighing things up. "Does a heat pump make sense in a house from 1975?"
- Questions asking for a recommendation. "Who can help me with the books of a limited company with staff?"
- Questions with several parts. "I am looking for a landscaper near Ede who also does the maintenance and is not too expensive."
- Follow up questions. With an AI assistant you keep asking, with Google you start again.

For most companies that provide services, the valuable question is in the second list. Those are the questions that come just before an enquiry.

## Google is becoming an AI assistant too

There is another reason why "ChatGPT against Google" is not the right picture. Google is moving in the same direction.

The AI Overview above the search results is a written answer with a few sources. AI Mode is a conversation. Gemini, Google's assistant, is in phones and in Chrome. Anyone who puts a question to Google therefore also gets advice instead of a list more and more often.

So the shift is not from one company to another. It is from searching to asking. That is happening everywhere at once. What to do about it in Google is covered in [strengthen my position on Google](#art_mijn_positie_versterken_op_google).

## What does this mean for your company?

Three conclusions you can use tomorrow.

### 1. Do not stop with Google

It is tempting to put everything on AI because it is new. Do not. Most searching still happens at Google, and your business profile and your regular findability remain the foundation for local customers. Besides, AI assistants themselves use search engines to build their answer. If you cannot be found there, you drop out in AI as well.

### 2. Look beyond your position

A report that only shows your position for ten keywords now tells half the story. The other half is whether you are mentioned when someone asks a whole question. You have to measure that separately. How to do that yourself is covered in [is my company mentioned in AI](#art_mijn_bedrijf_genoemd_in_ai).

### 3. Write for the question, not for the keyword

This is the practical core. A page made to rank for "plumber Deventer" is something different from a page that explains what to do when you have a leak, what it costs and when you should call someone. The first works in a list. The second works in a list and in advice.

The nice thing is that you do not have to choose. Good explanation also ranks in Google. The difference between the two ways of working is covered in [what is GEO and how does it differ from SEO](#art_wat_is_geo_en_wat_is_het_verschil_met_seo).

## What is still uncertain

It would not be honest to pretend everything is clear. There are three open questions.

**Will there be adverts?** OpenAI has started with adverts in ChatGPT. How that relates to the regular answers, and whether you will soon be able to buy a place in a recommendation, has not yet settled. For now the content of the answer is not for sale.

**Will people keep trusting it?** AI assistants make mistakes. If users receive poor advice a few times, use for this kind of question may level off. So far we do not see that.

**Who will be the largest?** ChatGPT is ahead, but Gemini has the advantage of sitting inside Google's products, and Claude is growing in business use. Nobody knows what that will look like in two years.

Precisely because this is uncertain, we advise against aiming at one system. The systems search in different places, as you can see in [Gemini does not mention my company](#art_gemini_noemt_mijn_bedrijf_niet) and [Claude uses Brave to find your company](#art_claude_gebruikt_brave_om_jouw_bedrijf_te_vinden). What they have in common: they can only mention what they can read.

## A sober look at the hurry

We see two reactions among business owners. One group acts as if nothing is going on. The other group panics and wants to overhaul everything tomorrow. Neither is needed.

What is going on is a gradual shift. Each quarter a slightly larger share of your customers puts the question to an AI assistant. You do not notice it from one day to the next. You notice it in two years, when it turns out that the competitor who was early is mentioned for all those questions and you are not.

There is also a reassuring side. Most companies have not done anything about this yet. Whoever starts now is not starting late. They are starting ahead of the rest.

> The question is not whether ChatGPT replaces Google. The question is whether your customer hears your name, wherever they ask.

## One customer, two routes

To make the difference concrete, follow the same customer twice. She wants solar panels on a house with a roof that faces east and west.

In the first route she uses a search engine. She types a few words, opens four websites, reads a comparison article and fills in two quote forms. Two installers call her back. She had seen eight names along the way and picked two more or less on gut feeling.

In the second route she asks an AI assistant whether panels pay off on an east and west roof. She asks what a system costs for her usage. She asks which installers nearby have experience with that kind of roof. She gets three names with a sentence about each. She calls the first.

The same customer, the same need, the same town. In the first route eight companies had a chance. In the second route three did. Neither route is going away, which is exactly why you want to be present in both.

## In summary

- ChatGPT is not the new Google. It is a second place where people search, and that place is growing fast.
- The difference is in the answer: a list with ten options versus advice with three names.
- Google itself is moving in the same direction with AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- For a business owner it means: keep doing Google, measure AI alongside it and write for questions.
